Terms of use

Phantocite checks whether the sources cited in a paper exist and whether they are cited for what they actually say. Using it means accepting the terms below.

Last updated 9 September 2026.

1. What this tool is

Phantocite is a free aid for checking references. It looks each reference up in OpenAlex, compares the printed details with that record, and asks a language model whether each citing sentence matches the abstract of the source.

It is an aid, not an authority. It produces leads for a human reviewer. It is not a plagiarism service, not a grading instrument, and not evidence of academic misconduct. Any decision about a person must rest on your own reading of the sources, never on this output alone.

3. Accuracy — no warranty

The tool is provided as is, with no warranty of any kind. In particular:

  • It produces false positives and false negatives. A real work can be reported as missing when OpenAlex indexes it oddly or not at all; a fabricated one can occasionally match something.
  • The misquote judgement is made from the abstract only. A claim that the full text supports but the abstract does not may be flagged wrongly.
  • Metadata differences often reflect the record, not the paper — an online-first year against an issue year, for example.
  • Long documents may be checked only in part, within the server's time budget, and are marked as partial when that happens.

Verify every flag by hand before you act on it.

4. What happens to your document

  • The language model provider receives the document's text, in order to find the reference list and the citing sentences, and receives the citation snippets and OpenAlex abstracts for the misquote check.
  • OpenAlex receives reference titles and DOIs only — never the paper's prose, and never a name from the document.
  • Nothing is stored. There is no database and no file storage. Your document exists in memory for the length of the run and is then gone. Results live in your browser only; downloads are produced from what is already on your screen.
  • Analytics (Google Analytics, after you accept cookies) records page views and usage, never your document, references or results.

Hosting and network providers may keep their own short-lived request logs, which record the fact of a request rather than its content.
